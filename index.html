AquaSentinel imports three registers from a fictional water utility — assets, approvals, observations — and lists where they disagree. Every finding names the imported rows that produced it. It never says anything about exposure, severity or compromise.

Load the golden fixture in the workbench →

What it does

  • Validates one JSON envelope (aqua-demo-1) atomically: any error rejects the whole import and the previous dataset stays.
  • Runs the fictional rule pack aqua-review-1: UNKNOWN_ASSET, NO_APPROVAL, OUTSIDE_APPROVAL_WINDOW, AMBIGUOUS_APPROVAL, OWNER_MISSING, STALE_OBSERVATION.
  • Freezes the report; lets a reviewer add notes and dispositions without touching imported evidence.
  • Exports JSON (exact data, source hash, versions) and formula-safe CSV.

What it does not do

  • No scanning, fetching, DNS lookups, device connections, PLC writes or remediation. URL-like identifiers are plain text.
  • No real infrastructure data. Fixtures are labelled synthetic; the only source of the rules is the slice specification.
  • No legal, regulatory, clinical or security-assurance claim. A missing observation never implies safety.
  • No accounts, tenants, uploads or deployment. Served on 127.0.0.1 only.

Bounds

1 MiB input · 1,000 assets · 2,000 approvals · 5,000 observations · 200-character identifiers · 2,000-character notes · nesting depth 8 · 10 MiB export. Timestamps must be real UTC calendar instants ending in Z; identifiers keep their exact text after ASCII-whitespace trimming, and trimming collisions are rejected.
